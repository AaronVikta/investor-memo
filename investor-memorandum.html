00Introduction

Signature Private Cooperative is the trading name of Signature Private Multipurpose Cooperative Society Limited, a Nigerian cooperative society (Registration No. ABSG/MOPASP/2026/00343) that pools member capital to acquire real estate, businesses, and agricultural assets, then divides each one into units members hold directly. This document exists to answer, with evidence rather than assertion, the questions that actually decide whether a claim like that is real: does the software do what the marketing site says it does; does the money move safely; is the legal structure coherent; and where, honestly, is the platform still short of where it eventually needs to be.

Every section below either cites a specific file and line in the running system, quotes a real legal document the Society has already published, or says plainly that it couldn't find backing for a claim. Nothing here is a projection.

01Executive Summary

The Problem

Real-asset investment in Nigeria sits at two extremes. Informal cooperative and contribution schemes (ajo/esusu-adjacent property syndicates, family land arrangements) move real money among real people but carry none of the operational rigor a member would want if their capital sat there for years — no audit trail, no independent valuation, no dispute process. At the other extreme, SEC-regulated collective investment schemes and REITs bring that rigor but are built for a different audience and a different minimum ticket size, and they structure the investor as a retail shareholder in an instrument, not as a member of a community they were personally vetted into.

Signature's own positioning, stated on its marketing site, names this gap directly: the platform is built to be “real assets, not speculation” and “evidence, not promises” — every asset carries an independent valuation and title documents before its allocation opens, and distributions are shown as a worked example, never a projected return marketing-site/src/pages/About.tsx:24-44.

The Cooperative Model

Signature is not structured as a fund, a brokerage, or an SEC-registered investment scheme. It is a registered cooperative society under the Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act 98 of 2004, and its own governing membership agreement is explicit about what that means for a member's legal position:

“Members explicitly acknowledge that they are not ‘passive retail investors’ buying ‘shares.’… Members are co-owners of cooperative capital units.” Membership Agreement, Article II.2 — crates/compliance/assets/membership_agreement_v1.md

Capital contributions are tracked internally as Cooperative Asset Units, not debt instruments, public equity, or deposit liabilities; what a member receives back is a Patronage Refund / Surplus Distribution, calculated from actual net performance — not a guaranteed yield membership_agreement_v1.md, Article III. The clearest single statement of why this structure was chosen over a securities-style one appears in the separate agreement used for unreferred applicants:

“The Society is a Private Cooperative Multipurpose Society operating under applicable state/federal Cooperative Laws, and is NOT a public limited liability company, stockbroker, bank, or fund manager registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) or the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN).” Walk-In Agreement, Article 3.1 — crates/compliance/assets/walk_in_agreement_v1.md

Why Now

The evidence gathered for this document reads as a platform past its initial build-out and into a hardening phase: recent engineering work concentrates on closing money-integrity race conditions, encrypting PII at rest, remediating dependency CVEs, and banning unsafe error-handling patterns outright — the signature of a team treating this as infrastructure that will hold real capital, not a demo (full detail in Roadmap · Current Status). Combined with a compliance and financial-controls layer (dual control, AML structuring detection, DB-enforced immutable ledgers) that most platforms this size don't build until forced to, the operational case for admitting members now is that the plumbing has already been built to the standard that matters later, not one that needs retrofitting under pressure.

Scope note

02Proof of Concept

Five questions, answered from the actual running system: what exists end to end, how it's secured, how money is protected in motion, how identity is verified, and how the whole thing stays up. Scale, for context: 21 backend crates, 92 database migrations, 641 test functions, 179 registered API routes, a real Postgres 16 instance in CI — not a mocked one — and two automated gates blocking every pull request: cargo audit and a custom scanner that fails a build if a secret or personal data reaches a log line.

  • Ownership & holdings — member portfolio and cap-table views. crates/ownership

Framing

“Live” here means route, service, and database schema exist and are exercised by tests — it is evidence the system works as designed, not evidence of real production transaction volume. That distinction matters and this document doesn't blur it.

Security & Audit

Two facts anchor this subsection. First: the ledger and every other financially or legally significant table is immutable at the database level, not just by application convention — a Postgres trigger rejects any UPDATE or DELETE against audit_logs, ledger_entries, trades, revenue_distributions, revenue_allocations, platform_fee_ledger, diligence_reviews, and every legal-document/agreement table, enforced even against someone holding the raw database credentials migrations/0044_immutable_ledger_tables.sql. Second: the codebase carries 80 comments literally labeled “Security-audit fix” grep -rn "Security-audit fix" crates, each describing a real, dated remediation — a representative sample:

  • Account lockout after 10 failed logins in 15 minutes, distinct from IP-based rate limiting. crates/auth/src/service.rs:88-92 (fix M3)
  • A dummy password hash is checked on every login attempt for an unknown email, closing a response-timing side channel that would otherwise reveal which emails have accounts. crates/auth/src/service.rs:100-106
  • The API docs endpoint is gated behind constant-time HTTP Basic auth and fails closed in production. crates/app/src/docs_auth.rs:1-40 (fix M4)
  • Boot-time validation rejects placeholder JWT secrets and requires the access and refresh secrets to differ and be at least 32 bytes. crates/shared/src/config.rs:403-430 (fix L6)
  • Actor attribution in the audit log is deliberately written to avoid blaming an affected member for an action staff took on their behalf. crates/audit/src/repository.rs:24-50 (fix F-13)

Beyond these: identity documents and bank account numbers are encrypted at rest with ChaCha20-Poly1305 (authenticated encryption, a fresh random nonce per value) crates/shared/src/encryption.rs:1-80; permissions are enforced through a database-backed role/permission model on every route, not scattered conditionals crates/rbac; every meaningful action across roughly nine crates lands in the same immutable audit log; rate limiting is IP-keyed on public routes and account-keyed on authenticated ones, aware of proxy spoofing crates/app/src/rate_limit.rs; and a full security-header suite (default-deny CSP, one-year HSTS, frame denial) is verified by an automated test that fails the build if any header goes missing crates/app/src/security_headers.rs:64-94.

Named, not hidden, limitation

Dual control (see Financial Controls) proves two different staff accounts approved an action. It cannot cryptographically prove two different people did — a single individual holding two admin-capable accounts could defeat it. The code says so directly rather than implying a stronger guarantee than it has. crates/wallets/src/service.rs:799-816

Honest framing

These 80 fix comments are strong evidence of an active, ongoing internal review discipline — they are not, by themselves, a signed independent audit report. If Signature holds a third-party audit engagement outside this codebase, it should be cited by name alongside this evidence, not conflated with it.

Financial Controls

Every deposit, withdrawal, marketplace order, and revenue distribution accepts an idempotency key, so a retried or double-submitted request is deduplicated rather than double-processed crates/wallets/src/service.rs:38. Withdrawal confirmation uses a compare-and-swap claim: the request atomically moves from pending to processing, and only the caller that wins that transition actually calls the payment provider — the loser is told the request is already being handled, never allowed to submit it a second time crates/wallets/src/service.rs:824-838. When a payout's outcome is genuinely unclear after a network failure, the system asks the provider what actually happened rather than guessing, and leaves the request for staff review if the answer is still ambiguous — deliberately avoiding both a false failure and a double-payment crates/wallets/src/service.rs:864-949.

Deposit webhooks from Paystack and Monnify are verified twice before a wallet is ever credited: first the HMAC signature over the raw request body, then an independent re-confirmation against the provider's own verify API — the payload itself is never trusted on its own crates/wallets/src/api.rs:443-514, 477-480. A reconciliation job runs every fifteen minutes comparing the internal ledger against the real balance held at the live payment gateway, and only escalates a discrepancy to staff after it has persisted across three consecutive runs, so ordinary settlement lag doesn't trigger false alarms crates/wallets/src/reconciliation.rs:72-79.

Precision, not overclaim

Which payment rail is live — manual bank transfer, Paystack, or Monnify — is a staff-editable configuration value, not a fixed architectural choice. The database is currently seeded to Monnify migrations/0067_active_payment_provider.sql:6-14, which supports describing payment rails as genuinely live today, but this is an operational fact worth re-confirming per environment, not a permanent guarantee. A misconfigured deployment silently falls back to manual bookkeeping rather than failing loudly.

Compliance & KYC

KYC auto-approves only when three independent conditions all hold: a verification provider is configured, a real-time National Identification Number (NIN) lookup succeeds and that NIN isn't already claimed by another account, and the verified legal name plausibly matches the applicant's account name. If any one of those fails, the submission is left Pending for a staff reviewer — it never auto-rejects crates/compliance/src/service.rs:151-249. A member's self-declared status as a Politically Exposed Person routes directly into a real staff AML review queue rather than a form field nobody reads crates/compliance/src/aml.rs:26-38, and a separate, config-tunable structuring-detection job watches for the deposit pattern regulators associate with money laundering (many small deposits under a threshold in a rolling window) and flags it for review, race-safe under concurrent writes crates/compliance/src/aml.rs.

Unverified integration detail

NIN verification calls Monnify's identity-lookup API against Nigeria's NIMC database, and the code's own comment is direct about a limitation: the exact shape of a successful response beyond one confirmed field is documented as “a best guess… not confirmed against a real response as of writing.” crates/compliance/src/monnify.rs:4-16 This should be confirmed against real production traffic before being cited elsewhere as a fully verified integration.

Infrastructure & Uptime

The container runs as a non-root user from a multi-stage build, with a health check baked into the image itself Dockerfile:1-41. Two health endpoints exist for different purposes: /health is a plain liveness check, and /health/ready runs a real query against Postgres, letting a load balancer drain traffic during a database outage rather than restarting a healthy process crates/app/src/main.rs:2864-2877. The production container itself runs with a read-only root filesystem, every Linux capability dropped, and its port bound to loopback only, implying a reverse proxy in front docker-compose.backend.yml:1-23. Error monitoring (Sentry) is opt-in via an environment variable, paired deliberately with the log-safety scanner mentioned above, since enabling it means log lines now reach infrastructure outside the company's own servers.

Precision, not overclaim

Production deployment is a deliberate, manually-triggered promotion — not deployment on every merge. CI (formatting, linting, the full test suite against a real database, dependency-vulnerability scanning, log-safety scanning) runs on every pull request; going live is a separate, one-click, human-triggered action that polls the new container's health before declaring success. That's a controlled-release posture, arguably a better trust story than full continuous deployment — but it should be described accurately as that, not as “automated CI/CD.”

03Mathematical Modeling

The actual arithmetic the platform runs on — shown, not just described, since a document about proof shouldn't ask a reader to take formulas on faith either.

Fractional Ownership Mechanics

Every asset carries a database-checked invariant tying its total valuation to its units:

// crates/assets/src/domain.rs:171-175, DB-checked
total_valuation_minor = total_units × unit_price_minor

An asset moves through a strict lifecycle — Draft → UnderReview → Listed → Funded → Closed — governed by a single transition table rather than scattered status checks, and the move from Listed to Funded fires automatically the moment a raise fills crates/assets/src/domain.rs:47-138. Committing capital converts it to units through a service call that is KYC-gated, requires at least one unit, and blocks an asset's own creator from investing in their own raise crates/investments/src/service.rs:62-192. Two guards are enforced at the database transaction level, not just in application code:

  • Oversubscription is structurally impossible — the commit is an atomic conditional update (WHERE units_committed + $1 <= total_units) under a row lock, so two concurrent commitments can never together oversell an asset. crates/investments/src/repository.rs:174-187
  • A per-member ownership cap is checked both before the commit and again inside the locked transaction, closing the race a pre-check alone would leave open — full consolidation of an asset by one member through ordinary trading is, by design, unreachable. crates/ownership/src/cap.rs:12-45
Named limit

A raise is strictly all-or-nothing: it only converts to holdings once units_committed == total_units exactly crates/investments/src/repository.rs:323. There is no partial or minimum-viable-raise threshold anywhere in the code today.

Returns & Yield Distribution

Pro-rata payout uses the largest-remainder method: each holder first receives the floor of their proportional share, computed in 128-bit intermediate arithmetic to avoid overflow, and the handful of leftover minor units left over from flooring are handed out one each to the holders with the largest fractional remainders — guaranteeing the total paid out sums exactly to the pool, never a kobo over or under, verified by an assertion at both the calculation and the write site crates/revenue/src/allocation.rs:1-67, crates/revenue/src/repository.rs:546-550. The pool itself is computed before that split:

// crates/revenue/src/repository.rs:537-539
holder_pool_minor = total_amount_minor − platform_fee_minor − vat_minor − wht_minor

Fee, VAT, and withholding-tax rates are staff-configured percentages, capped so their combined total can never exceed 100% crates/revenue/src/service.rs:175-179. Distributions tied to an asset sale instead apply capital-gains tax to each holder's own individual profit, never a flat fee — the two mechanisms are mutually exclusive per distribution crates/revenue/src/service.rs:153-185. Wallet crediting happens synchronously, in the same database transaction as the distribution itself, and above a configurable threshold the distribution requires a second, different staff approver before it can execute at all — the same dual-control pattern used for withdrawals crates/revenue/src/service.rs:415-521.

Marketing example doesn't match the real arithmetic

The homepage's own worked example — “an asset earns ₦500,000 in rent, you hold 10 of 200 units (5%), you receive ₦25,000” — runs a clean straight percentage of the gross amount marketing-site/src/pages/Home.tsx:511-515. In reality, platform fee, VAT, and withholding tax are deducted from the gross before the pro-rata split, so a member's real share is computed off a smaller net pool. The page's existing disclaimer covers performance variability, not this specific gap. Worth fixing in the marketing copy directly — see Claims & Their Proof.

Precision on “automatically”

The marketing site's use of “automatically” describes the wallet-crediting mechanics — instant, same-transaction — not an automatic trigger the moment revenue is collected. Distribution itself is a deliberate action a staff member takes, gated by permission and, above a threshold, a second approver.

Risk Model

Assets carry real risk-disclosure fields — a risk level, a plain-language key-risk summary, valuer name/date/methodology, and outstanding-debt disclosure — added deliberately, each defaulting to an honest “not yet assessed” state rather than a silent default crates/assets/src/domain.rs:214-223. The member-facing UI reflects that honesty directly: when no risk summary has been published, it says so (“Risk not yet assessed”) instead of hiding the field or inventing one investor-app/src/pages/AssetDetail.tsx:275-291.

Real, current gap

There is no whole-asset write-down, impairment, or negative-distribution mechanism in the system today — a distribution must always be a positive amount crates/revenue/src/service.rs:96-100. The only signed-loss concept that exists is a realized loss on an individual secondary-market trade, where capital-gains tax is floored at zero but a seller's own profit figure can be negative crates/marketplace/src/domain.rs:91-93. If an asset's value genuinely falls, the model has no built-in way to reflect that to holders yet. This is a real product limitation, not a documentation gap, and this document names it as one.

Cooperative Pool Dynamics

Staff can cancel an entire raise, which atomically refunds every committed member in one transaction crates/investments/src/service.rs:211-250; an individual member can independently cancel their own still-open commitment before the raise fills, closed off the moment it does crates/investments/src/service.rs:257-284. Secondary trading happens only inside staff-opened exit windows, never continuously — a sell order escrows the seller's units the moment it's placed, and a trade settles buyer, seller, units, and money together in one atomic step or not at all crates/marketplace/src/domain.rs:6-46. Three further guards, all staff-configurable: a holder lock-in period before newly-acquired units can be resold (present in code, disabled by default) crates/marketplace/src/service.rs:203-220,432; a looser secondary-market ownership cap reusing the same concentration-limit logic as the raise-time cap crates/marketplace/src/service.rs:301-311; and a price-ceiling guard bounding how far above the raise or last-trade price a sell order can be listed crates/marketplace/src/service.rs:444-451.

The dual-control threshold pattern used for withdrawals reappears, deliberately reused rather than reinvented, for revenue-entry approval — the two implementations explicitly cross-reference each other in their own code comments crates/wallets/src/service.rs:349,708-814, crates/revenue/src/service.rs:415-476, a small but real signal this was designed as one system rather than patched feature by feature.

04Legal & Governance

This section touches real legal exposure directly, so it stays strictly to what the actual governing documents and code say — and names plainly what would need separate counsel review rather than asserting it.

Cooperative Structure

Admission is by referral or Board sponsorship only, and the Board holds “absolute, non-appealable discretion” over acceptance membership_agreement_v1.md, Article I. The agreement itself isn't a hardcoded copy baked into the frontend — it's server-generated text, content-hashed with SHA-256 at the time a member accepts it, and that hash is never accepted as a client-supplied value crates/compliance/src/membership_agreement.rs:11-20, so what a member sees and what's recorded as accepted are provably the same text. A mandatory asset-specific lock-up applies to every contribution — “early redemption or liquidation requests are not permitted” — the legal counterpart to the marketplace's own lock-in configuration membership_agreement_v1.md, Article V.1.

A separate Walk-In Agreement governs applicants who arrive without a personal referrer: it grants only Provisional Candidate Status, explicitly not automatic admission, and bars any financial transaction during candidacy until the Society's Executive Desk / Board of Trustees formally sponsors them walk_in_agreement_v1.md, Article 2.

Member Rights & Obligations

Beyond the transfer and lock-up terms above, the agreement carries broad indemnification of the Society, its trustees, and developers, with a member's own liability capped at twelve months of their own admin fees paid membership_agreement_v1.md, Article VI; disputes route through internal Board review, then binding arbitration under Co-operative Societies Law or the Lagos Court of Arbitration's rules Article VII.

The strongest concrete evidence of real member power sits in the governance system, not the legal text alone: a proposal to sell an asset must actually pass a quorum-then-majority, unit-weighted vote — with each vote snapshotted at cast time so a later trade can't retroactively shift the outcome — before staff can even record the resulting sale proceeds as revenue crates/governance/src/domain.rs:105-128, crates/revenue/src/service.rs:356-389. That's a structural gate on asset disposal, not an advisory poll.

Regulatory Positioning (Nigeria)

The Society's position is stated identically and directly in two places — a governing legal document and the public marketing site:

“This platform is a proprietary administrative interface operating exclusively for registered members of Signature Private Multipurpose Cooperative Society Limited, registered under the Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act 98 of 2004 (Registration No. ABSG/MOPASP/2026/00343). We do not solicit funds from the general public, issue securities, or offer investment advisory services.” marketing-site/src/pages/Trust.tsx:67-76

The Nigeria Data Protection Act is cited substantively in the Society's privacy policy — both a retention-disclosure obligation and the concrete data-subject rights it grants (access, correction, deletion, objection, portability) crates/compliance/assets/privacy_policy_v1.md:77,85, and those rights aren't just stated: the self-service export and erasure endpoints referenced in Compliance & KYC operationalize them directly.

Sourcing gap

No citation to the Companies and Allied Matters Act (CAMA) appears anywhere in the codebase, migrations, or legal documents reviewed for this report. If a CAMA-specific compliance claim is ever needed, it requires separate legal sourcing — nothing here backs it, and nothing here should be read as implying it.

Asset Custody & Title

The trust structure is embedded in a real generated legal artifact, not just marketing prose. Every Certificate of Unit Ownership PDF a member can download carries a hardcoded trust statement:

“The underlying asset is held in trust by Signature Private Multipurpose Cooperative Society Limited (Reg. No. ABSG/MOPASP/2026/00343) as trustee for the members who capitalized it, in proportion to their Cooperative Asset Units.” crates/ownership/src/certificate.rs:74-80, rendered on every certificate at :334-346

— alongside an explicit disclaimer that the certificate is not itself a title deed. A member only ever receives an actual title-deed-class document if they consolidate 100% of a single asset's units, gated by a direct equality check between units held and total units outstanding crates/ownership/src/service.rs:127-160; for the platform's actual fractional product, this Certificate plus its trust statement is the only title-adjacent document any member ever gets — which matches what the marketing site says precisely.

Real, worth-understanding nuance

The database does not model “the Society” as a distinct legal titleholder entity. An asset's owner_id points to an individual staff/deal-originator's profile, not a dedicated trust-deed or legal-entity record — the code's own comments note an earlier feature that could have represented the Society as an entity was retired crates/assets/src/domain.rs:28-41. In practice this is normal: real trusts are usually a paper/registry fact, not something every system needs a database row for. But the trustee claim currently rests entirely on consistent process and generated document text, not on a first-class data record proving it — the document shouldn't overclaim “database-enforced trust structure,” and doesn't.

Factual discrepancy — worth resolving directly

The Society's registered address as stated in its own binding Membership Agreement is “No. 58 Macaulay by Ikot Ekpene Road…” membership_agreement_v1.md, Article Recitals. Every public-facing surface — the marketing site's Contact page, its structured data, and config.ts — instead says “No. 68 Macaulay…” marketing-site/src/pages/Contact.tsx:91, src/config.ts:18. One of these two numbers is wrong. This is exactly the kind of small, checkable inconsistency worth fixing before this document (or the legal document itself) goes in front of anyone external.

Article 5.2 of the Membership Agreement describes unit transfers as Board-approval-gated, member-to-member only, with a written notice and admin fee. The actual marketplace implementation is an automated, intra-Society exit-window market instead — the same policy intent (transfers stay inside the membership) is preserved, but the mechanism described in the legal text and the mechanism actually running are not identical. Both should be described accurately going forward rather than treated as interchangeable.

05Roadmap

Current Status

Payment rails follow a deliberately conservative rollout: Paystack is fully wired (hosted checkout, dual-control payout, webhook verification, live balance reconciliation); Monnify is the currently active provider per the live database configuration; Flutterwave has no implementation yet; manual (staff-confirmed bank transfer) remains the safe fallback any misconfiguration lands on. A genuinely sophisticated, staff-tunable operations layer is already live and not just planned — AML structuring-detection thresholds, dual-control thresholds for both withdrawals and revenue entries, per-member ownership caps on both raises and marketplace trades, and separate VAT/CGT/withholding-tax rates are all configuration values a staff member can change without a deploy crates/admin/src/config_service.rs.

The shape of recent engineering work across all three repositories reads as a genuine post-launch hardening phase, not early build-out: closing money-integrity race conditions in ownership caps and withdrawal/revenue processing, encrypting bank account numbers at rest, banning unchecked error-unwrapping outright via a compiler lint, and remediating a dependency CVE. There is no informal TODO backlog scattered through the code — what remains unfinished is documented formally, in the backend's own README and in doc comments, which is itself a signal of discipline worth noting.

What's Next

Taken directly from the backend's own documented list of known gaps, rather than reconstructed from outside:

  • API documentation currently covers 49 of 133 routes; the rest (assets, governance, documents, admin, analytics, notifications, audit, revenue) aren't yet annotated.
  • The internal event bus is in-process only; a durable transport (e.g. Kafka or NATS) is the planned replacement once that guarantee needs to become absolute.
  • Exit windows and governance proposals are opened and closed manually by staff — the scheduling infrastructure for deadline-driven auto-close exists, but isn't wired up yet.
  • Holder lock-in is implemented but ships disabled by default (zero days).
  • Automatic refund on a raise that times out unfilled needs a background job scheduler and is explicitly deferred; a manual staff cancel is today's workaround.
  • No Flutterwave integration yet.
Scope note

This captures what the code itself signals is unfinished. Any forward-looking business roadmap beyond this — new asset classes, timelines, features not yet started in code — needs to come from the team directly; nothing here should be read as inventing one.

07FAQ

Reproduced from the platform's own live FAQ page, which already carries structured data for search — extended only where this deeper document answers something the public page doesn't need to.

Who can become a member?

Membership is by referral only, from an existing KYC-verified member or staff. There's no public signup, and referring someone in earns no commission.

What's the minimum to start?

There's no fixed minimum — unit prices vary by asset, and how much capital you commit is simply however many units you choose above that per-unit price.

How does holding units actually work?

Two ways: revenue distribution when the asset earns (rent, business income, harvest), split pro-rata per the cooperative's bylaws; and resale gain or loss if you sell your units in a scheduled exit window.

Can I exit whenever I want?

No. Liquidity is deliberately bounded to scheduled exit windows rather than pretending a real, illiquid asset trades like a listed stock minute to minute.

Is my money safe while it sits in my wallet?

It sits on an audited, append-only ledger that reconciles exactly, and can only ever be withdrawn to your own verified bank account.

What happens if an allocation doesn't fill?

Every committed member is refunded automatically and in full — there is no minimum-raise threshold below which the platform proceeds anyway.

Do I get a say in the assets I own?

Yes — governance votes weighted by the units you hold in that specific asset, and for a sale proposal specifically, that vote is a real gate: staff cannot record sale proceeds until it passes.

Who holds the actual asset?

Signature Private Multipurpose Cooperative Society Limited, as trustee for the members who capitalized it — not any individual member, and not the platform operator personally.

Do I get a Certificate of Ownership or a Title Deed?

You get a personal Certificate of Unit Ownership recording your units, acquisition date, and cost basis — it is explicitly not a title deed. A literal title-deed-class document is only ever issued if you consolidate 100% of one asset's units.

08Glossary

Cooperative / Society

Signature Private Cooperative is the trading name of Signature Private Multipurpose Cooperative Society Limited, registered under the Nigerian Co-operative Societies Act 98 of 2004. A cooperative society, not a fund or a brokerage.

Unit

The fractional ownership instrument. An asset is divided into a fixed number of units at a fixed, server-computed price.

Holding

One line of a member's portfolio — their position in one asset: units held, the current market-anchor price, and their own real weighted-average cost basis.

Raise

The fundraising/allocation period for one asset. Ends when the last unit is committed (all-or-nothing) or when staff cancel it, refunding everyone.

Exit window

A staff-opened, time-bounded secondary-market window in which members may list units for sale. Outside a window, there is no trading.

Pro-rata distribution

Revenue split proportionally to units held, net of platform fee/VAT/WHT, using the largest-remainder method so the total paid always sums exactly.

Certificate of Unit Ownership

A personal record of units held, acquisition date, and cost basis in an asset — not a title deed.

Trust / trustee

Signature holds legal title to each real asset as trustee for the members who capitalized it; a member's claim is a beneficial interest under that trust, not direct title.

KYC (Know Your Customer)

Identity verification required before a member can contribute capital or withdraw. Deposits are allowed before approval; moving money isn't.

PEP (Politically Exposed Person)

Self-declared at KYC submission. Never auto-rejects a member — it gives staff something concrete to weigh at review.

Dual control

Above a configurable amount, a second, different staff member must independently approve a withdrawal or revenue entry before it executes.

Walk-in candidate / Walk-in agreement

A separate legal document and status for applicants admitted without a personal referrer — provisional only, sponsored by the Board directly, not automatic admission.

General admission code

A non-personal, server-owned referral code staff can issue for the walk-in path, distinct from a member's own individual invite code.
